Refining the detection
of SLX effects.

The SLX model adds spatially lagged explanatory variables to a regression. It allows an outcome in one unit to be related to the characteristics of other units connected through a spatial weights matrix.

This paper studies the score test for these effects. Its central question is: which part of the SLX test is already contained in classical spatial diagnostics, and what additional information remains?

Marcos Herrera-Gómez / CONICET–CIANECO, UNRC

Background / Models and diagnostics

The SLX model and the spatial alternatives.

Section 2 starts from a linear regression without spatial components and considers three alternatives. All score statistics are evaluated at the same ordinary least squares (OLS) fit.

Reading the notation: y is the outcome, X contains the explanatory variables and the intercept, and β contains their coefficients. W is the spatial weights matrix. Xnc excludes the intercept; WXnc contains the spatially lagged explanatory variables.

SLM / Spatial lag

A lagged dependent variable

y=ρWy+Xβ+ε

Wy is the spatial lag of the outcome; ρ governs this channel.

SEM / Spatial error

Dependence in the errors

y=Xβ+u
u=λWu+ε

λ governs spatial dependence in the error term.

SLX / Spatial lag of X

Lagged explanatory variables

y=Xβ+WXncθ+ε

θ contains the coefficients on WXnc: the SLX effects.

The SLX diagnostic asks whether the restricted OLS fit already contains evidence of spatially lagged regressors, before an enlarged spatial model is estimated.

Which test examines each component?

Lagrange multiplier (LM) and Rao score (RS) refer to the same class of score tests here.

01 / The contribution

A test for SLX effects.
An exact decomposition to refine its reading.

Koley and Bera (2024) introduce RSθ to detect SLX effects. This paper shows how its statistic separates into two components with different interpretations.

RSθ=PSθ+OSθ

Common-factor component

PSθ is numerically identical to the classical robust lag test RSρ∗ in every sample. This direction alone cannot distinguish a spatially lagged dependent variable from an aligned SLX effect.

1 degree of freedom

Orthogonal component

OSθ captures the additional information supplied by the SLX branch beyond the classical battery.

k − 2 degrees of freedom

Failure to reject OSθ does not imply an absence of spillovers. Effects may remain aligned with the common-factor direction, θ=τβnc.

Source: Theorem 1, p. 9; Corollary 1, p. 10. k includes the intercept. At k = 2, OSθ is not defined.
02 / Interpreting the decomposition

A projection and an orthogonal remainder.

The common-factor direction

Under θ=τβnc, the spatially lagged regressors enter through one aggregated column. OSθ examines the remaining restrictions outside this direction.

04 / Inference

Exact algebra.
Inference under stated conditions.

Where does the decomposition come from?

It builds on Koley’s (2024) exact score identity:

Sρ=Sλ+β^nc′Sθ

The paper characterizes the surviving SLX component, OSθ, its distribution and its calibration. The identity uses the same W across components and Wι = ι.

What does “exact” mean?

The identity RSθ=PSθ+OSθ is algebraic, sample by sample. Under the complete null θ=ρ=λ=0, normal homoskedastic errors and the paper’s rank conditions, OSθn follows Beta(q2,n−k−q2), where q = k−2.

OFθ=n−k−qq·OSθn−OSθ

The equivalent statistic follows F(q, n−k−q). Without normality, the paper establishes an asymptotic χ2 law with q degrees of freedom under its stated assumptions.

What limits the empirical interpretation?

Spatial error dependence can distort calibration. The swλ correction addresses this channel, but not heteroskedasticity. The BP diagnostic rejects homoskedasticity in four of the five applications.

OSθ is an omnibus test: it does not identify which covariate carries the specific spillover content.

Source: Sections 3–5 and 7; Proposition 5, p. 10.
